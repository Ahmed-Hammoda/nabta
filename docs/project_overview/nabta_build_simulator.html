Raspberry Pi pins used

Check before the first power-on

  1. On the relay board, remove the JD-VCC jumper. VCC goes to Pi 3.3 V (pin 17); JD-VCC goes to the 5 V adapter. The relay coils then never load the Pi.
  2. The 5 V adapter's + never touches a Pi 5 V pin. Only the grounds are joined (the back and front GND rails are linked).
  3. With the pump adapter on, measure the Pi end of the flow wire (row 44): it must stay below 3.3 V. If the yellow wire reads 0 V when idle, the sensor has no pull-up: replace the divider with a 10 kΩ pull-up to 3.3 V.
  4. Diode band (cathode) goes to pump + (row 56), not to GND.
  5. Some 830-point breadboards have rails split in the middle (a gap in the red/blue line). If yours does, bridge each rail you use across the gap.
  6. The OLED pin order varies (some boards have VCC first). Follow the printed labels, not the position.
  7. Push the soil sensor into the soil only up to its line. Its electronics stay above the soil, inside the stake. Power it from 3.3 V, never 5 V, because the ADS1115 runs on 3.3 V.
  8. Turn on I²C (raspi-config), then i2cdetect -y 1 must show 23 3c 44 48.
  9. Relay inputs are on GPIO5/6, which are pulled high at boot, so the pump stays off while the Pi starts. Your code must set them HIGH (off) first.
  10. Leave about 10 cm of slack on the lid wires (screen and stake port) so the lid opens.

Small parts missing from the BOM

  • Resistors: 10 kΩ and 20 kΩ (or three 10 kΩ) for the flow-signal divider
  • About 10 male–male and 4 female–female jumpers (the BOM has only 40 male–female)
  • GX16 5-pin panel connector + plug (sensor stake port)
  • 1 m of 5-core cable (stake to base)
  • 3 rubber grommets: sealed wall, USB-C, barrel jack
  • Small perfboard for the splice inside the stake, heat-shrink, 22 AWG solid wire for the screw terminals
  • M2.5 standoffs and screws, PETG filament (plus clear PETG for the lantern)
  • Grow light: still not chosen. The relay CH2 output and the LIGHT socket are ready for it.

Tank: the sketch said about 1 L, but a 1 L tank with this footprint is only 3 cm deep, which is just what the submersible pump needs to stay under water. At about 2.5 L (8 cm deep) the tank gives about 1.5 L of usable water, or 10–15 doses.
