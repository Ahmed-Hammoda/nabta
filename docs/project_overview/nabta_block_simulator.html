Wiring diagram

Every pin from the 3D model, drawn as a schematic. A dot means a connection; lines that cross without a dot are not connected. Pin names match the silkscreen on each module. It redraws when you add a jar.

Wire list, in build order

One row per physical wire or part, in the order to build. Hover over a row to light up that wire in the 3D view. Breadboard rows are numbered 1–63 and holes a–j, as printed on the board. To extend later, add only the rows for the new jar block.

Raspberry Pi pins used

Check before the first power-on

    Small parts missing from the BOM

    • Resistors for each jar: 10 kΩ and 20 kΩ (or three 10 kΩ) for the flow-signal divider
    • Screw terminal block and 3 inline fuses (Pi 5 A, breadboard 2 A, lights sized to the strips)
    • USB-C lead with bare wires for the Pi; 18 AWG wire for the adapter and light lines
    • About 10 male–male and 4 female–female jumpers, plus 22 AWG solid wire for the screw terminals
    • One 5-core cable and one panel plug (GX16) per jar, plus 2-core wire for each light
    • Rubber grommets: hub back wall, sealed shelf, and the rail opening
    • Small perfboard for the splice in each stake, heat-shrink, M2.5 standoffs and screws
    • PETG filament, plus clear PETG for the stake lanterns

    The tank is 22 × 18 cm, filled to 11 cm: about 4 L. The float switch stops the pumps at about 1 L, which leaves about 3 L, or 30 doses of 100 mL shared between the jars.
