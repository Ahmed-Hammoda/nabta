NABTA - CHECK OF THE DECISION LIMITS AGAINST TRUSTED SOURCES
Checked: 16 Nov 2026. Limits checked: rules v2 (simulation_phase2/code/rules_v2.py and
simulation_phase1/bench/labels.py).

Verdicts:
  CONFIRMED      a trusted source gives the same value
  PARTLY         a source supports it, but the evidence is weak or indirect
  CHANGE?        the sources point to a different value (the team must decide)
  NOT CHECKABLE  no source can give this value; it must be measured on our pot
  DESIGN         our own engineering choice; say so in the paper

=====================================================================
1. AIR TEMPERATURE                                           CONFIRMED
=====================================================================
Our limits: optimal 15-25 C | acceptable 4-36 C | HEAT_DANGER above 36 | COLD_DANGER below 4

Source: the FAO ECOCROP data sheet for Mentha spicata var. crispa gives
  optimal min 15, optimal max 25, absolute min 4, absolute max 36  [R1]
  killing temperature: -8 C during rest, -1 C during early growth  [R1]
These are exactly our numbers, so the limits most likely came from ECOCROP.

Notes for the paper:
- ECOCROP lists the variety "crispa", not plain Mentha spicata. Say so.
- In a greenhouse, hydroponic study, spearmint fresh mass was highest at a mean daily
  temperature of 27.8-30.4 C [R2]. So 15-25 C is a conservative "optimal"; it is not
  the point of fastest growth.

=====================================================================
2. LIGHT (light_lux_hours_24h)                                 CHANGE?
=====================================================================
Our limits: optimal >= 285,000 lux-h | acceptable >= 90,000 | LOW_LIGHT below 90,000

Plant scientists measure light per day as the DLI (daily light integral) in mol/m2/day.
Conversion for sunlight: 1 lux = about 0.0185 umol/m2/s [R6], so
  DLI = lux-hours x 0.0185 x 3600 / 1,000,000 = lux-hours / 15,000
  285,000 lux-h = about 19 mol/m2/day
   90,000 lux-h = about  6 mol/m2/day

What the sources say:
- Mint fresh mass rose with DLI up to about 15-16 mol/m2/day, then fell [R3, R4].
- Mint is a "medium light" crop (10-20 mol/m2/day) [R4].
- Spearmint was studied at DLIs from about 5 to 19 mol/m2/day [R2].

Verdict:
- LOW_LIGHT below about 6 mol (90,000 lux-h): PARTLY. It is the bottom of the range studied.
- Optimal at 19 mol or more (285,000 lux-h): CHANGE? It is above the point where mint
  grows best (15-16 mol).
  Suggestion: optimal >= 225,000 lux-h (= 15 mol).
- The conversion holds for SUNLIGHT only. Under an LED grow light the factor is different
  (about 0.012-0.020) [R6], so the same lux reading means a different DLI.

=====================================================================
3. AIR HUMIDITY                                                 PARTLY
=====================================================================
Our limit: optimal 60-70 % (no alert, only the humidity_level label)

Source: Japanese mint (Mentha arvensis) grew well in a greenhouse kept at 60-70 % RH [R5].
No source gives a humidity optimum for spearmint itself. The evidence is weak, but the
risk is low, because humidity triggers no alert and no watering.

=====================================================================
4. SOIL MOISTURE (soil_moisture_index)                   NOT CHECKABLE
=====================================================================
Our limits: water at 70 or below | DRY_DANGER 25 or below | optimal 90-98 | TOO_WET 98 or above

Why no source can confirm these numbers:
- Our index is a sensor scale (dry air = 0, probe in water = 100). It is not the real
  water content of the soil.
- Capacitive probes must be calibrated for each soil or potting mix [R9, R10].

What the sources do give is a METHOD:
- FAO-56 Table 22, mint: depletion fraction p = 0.40 [R7]. This means water when 40 % of
  the available water has been used, where available water is the water between field
  capacity (FC) and wilting point (WP).
- In hot, dry weather, p is 10-25 % smaller [R7]. Water a little earlier.
- Spearmint tolerates drier soil better than peppermint and other mints [R8, R11].

How to set our limits from the sources (needs 2 measurements on the real pot, section 7):
  water threshold = FC - 0.40 x (FC - WP)
  DRY_DANGER      = at or near the WP reading
  TOO_WET         = above the FC reading (water standing in the soil)
Example: if FC reads 94 and WP reads 30, then 94 - 0.40 x 64 = 68.4.
That is close to our 70, but it must be measured, not assumed.

=====================================================================
5. FAULT LIMITS                                         DESIGN + DATASHEETS
=====================================================================
Sensor limits from the datasheets:
- BME280: works from -40 to 85 C and 0-100 % RH. Humidity accuracy is +-3 % only between
  20 and 80 % RH [R12].
- BH1750: measures 1-65,535 lux in its normal mode, with about +-20 % variation [R13].
  Direct sun can reach about 100,000 lux, so outdoors the sensor can stop at 65,535.

Our limits (all DESIGN; none comes from a plant source):
- SENSOR_ERROR if temperature is outside -10..60 C. This is narrower than what the BME280
  can measure; a pot in Tunis should never read outside it.
- SENSOR_ERROR if humidity is outside 1..100 %.
- SENSOR_ERROR if light is below 0.
- SENSOR_ERROR if soil is 2 or below (probe in air or unplugged). Not measured yet.
- SENSOR_ERROR if soil falls more than 15 points in 30 min; PUMP_FAULT if it rises more
  than 10 points with the pump off. Both use rates from our simulation, not measurements.
- TOO_WET at 98: DESIGN, chosen from the calibration (see RULES_v2.md).

7. WHAT TO TELL YOUR FRIEND (sensor side)
=====================================================================
A. Changes to the sensor code
 1. Calibration (agreed): index = (16650 - raw) / 9000 x 100, limited to 0-100.
 2. BH1750 in strong sun:
    - Use a mode or measurement-time setting that reads up to about 100,000 lux [R13].
    - If a reading is exactly 65535, log it as "saturated".
    - Otherwise the 24 h light sum reads too low outdoors, and the model gives a false
      LOW_LIGHT.
 3. light_lux_hours_24h:
    - Sum the readings over the last 24 h only: lux each minute / 60, over 1,440 minutes.
    - Save the sum to a file so it survives a reboot. Otherwise it starts again from 0
      and the model says LOW_LIGHT.
 4. Rows: read about once a minute; log the 30-minute average as one row.
 5. Same column names, units and rounding as now: soil 1 decimal, temp 2, humidity 1,
    lux now 1, lux-hours 2.
 6. Send numbers, not text, in the JSON to the model.
 7. Never hide or skip strange readings. Log what the sensor really reads.
 8. Keep the BME280 out of direct sun (in a shaded, ventilated spot). In the sun it
    reads much hotter than the air and gives false HEAT_DANGER alerts.
    (This is general practice for air-temperature sensors, not taken from R1-R13.)

B. Measurements needed (they set the soil limits, section 4)
 1. Unplugged probe: the reading with the probe unplugged, and with it in air.
 2. Field capacity (FC):
    - Water the pot until water runs out of the bottom.
    - Let it drain for 1-2 hours.
    - Record the reading.
 3. Wilting point (WP):
    - Let a pot dry with no watering, and record the reading when the mint starts to wilt.
    - If you cannot wait for that, record the reading when the soil is dry to the touch
      at 3-5 cm depth.
 4. Drying speed: points lost per hour on a warm day and on a cool day.
 5. Settle point: where the reading settles about 1 h after one normal pump run.
 6. Under a grow lamp: note the lamp model; the lux-to-DLI factor depends on it [R6].
